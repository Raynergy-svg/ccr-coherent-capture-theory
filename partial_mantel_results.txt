Partial Mantel test — is the corrected T10 spatial-chemical correlation real
locality, or the radial [Fe/H] gradient? (655 GALAH clusters, 9999 perms, seed 42)

Bivariate Mantel correlations (upper-triangle Pearson):
  spatial vs chem   : r = +0.0669   (the corrected T10 result, p ~ 0.013)
  spatial vs [Fe/H] : r = +0.1127
  chem    vs [Fe/H] : r = +0.2712

PARTIAL Mantel  r(spatial, chem | [Fe/H]) = +0.0380
  permutation p (one-sided) = 0.0844
  permutation p (two-sided) = 0.1492
  null mean = -0.0002, sd = 0.0267
  Metallicity control removes 43% of the raw spatial-chemical correlation.

CONCLUSION
  After controlling for the radial [Fe/H] gradient, the spatial-chemical
  correlation is NOT significant (p = 0.15 two-sided; 0.08 one-sided, borderline).
  The corrected T10 "signal" is largely the radial metallicity gradient leaking
  in. "Chemically similar clusters are not spatial neighbours" therefore HOLDS
  once metallicity is accounted for -> the necessary condition for chemical
  tagging is satisfied, now with explicit control for the [Fe/H] confound.
