# The missing discriminator

12 October 2026 • Theory-statement review only • No additional chemical outcomes read

## Finding

**No quantitative chemical prediction that distinguishes coherent capture from ordinary common-origin chemical tagging was found in the recovered original theory documents.** Consequently, better cluster membership or a stronger chemical correlation cannot, by itself, validate capture.

This conclusion is scoped to the sources actually recovered. It does not claim that no such prediction could ever be derived, or that an unavailable earlier document cannot contain one.

## What the sources do predict

- **Laws of Coherency**, internally dated 5 November 2025: preserved orbital alignment, eccentricity 0.05–0.10, resonance fraction below 0.3, few/no debris disks and obliquity above 15°. These are orbital/architectural assertions. No predicted C/O distribution, chemical effect size, or chemical comparison with a common-origin baseline is specified.
- **Final Theory Assessment**, internally dated 5 November 2025: a candidate classifier and proposed obliquity measurements. It supplies no capture-specific chemical prediction or calibrated likelihood against ordinary formation alternatives.
- **Design in Mechanism vs Randomness in Outcomes**, internally dated 14 November 2025: claims about velocity clustering, an optimal coherence value and system architectures. It supplies no quantitative chemical discriminator. Its premise that ordinary disk formation predicts no host-star velocity correlation is not a valid baseline when stars can form in the same cluster.
- The public [v3 theory statement, lines 27–43](https://github.com/Raynergy-svg/ccr-coherent-capture-theory/blob/ff273145c6edf855a810992d6f8afbdf7916a7de/coherent_capture_v3/THEORY_v3.md#L27-L43) proposes a dynamical coherence parameter and an orbital joint criterion. It is a later revision and contains no capture-specific chemical likelihood or effect size. Its physical derivation is not independently endorsed by this limited statement review.

The old documents' candidate percentages are classifier yields, not physical event rates. Their numerical orbital thresholds are not a derivation showing that realistic alternative formation histories could not produce the same observations. No newly recovered outcome is substituted to rescue those thresholds.

## Why common-origin chemistry is insufficient

Stars born together can exhibit chemical similarities without any planet changing its host. Chemical tagging was already an established research program before these CCT documents, including recovery of known stellar clusters. Membership-supported chemistry can therefore test a common-origin tagging claim while providing no unique support for planetary capture. [Kos et al. (2018)](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/473/4/4612/4555384)

Similarly, a planet–star atmospheric abundance mismatch would not automatically discriminate: the original documents do not supply a quantitative, uncertainty-aware prediction separating capture from disk chemistry, atmospheric evolution and measurement selection. A new mismatch threshold chosen after examining observations would not fill that gap.

## Stopping decision

**No further empirical CCT chemical test is proposed at this stage.** The membership result is preserved, but its newly defined subsets are not fed into another significance search.

The missing theoretical input is a pre-data specification of:

1. The exact measurable observable and population.
2. Its numerical predictive distribution under coherent capture, derived from declared physical assumptions.
3. Its predictive distribution under an explicit ordinary-formation/common-origin baseline, including relevant confounders and observation selection.
4. A difference large enough to resolve with stated measurement precision and a fixed rejection criterion.

If both models predict the same chemical coherence, that observable cannot discriminate between them regardless of sample size. A future test is justified only after such a difference is established; none is invented here.

Exact historical replication additionally needs the original classifier code, complete candidate tables, input snapshot and uncertainty treatment. These were not recovered. References in the later repository to earlier reports are not substitutes for the absent original artifacts.
